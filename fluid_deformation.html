Fluid Element Deformation & Vorticity

Angular velocity and vorticity in 2D flow  —  ME Lecture Visualization  —  flairsLab · IUT Dhaka

§1 — Motivation: a fluid element in the boundary layer
In a boundary layer the velocity increases away from the wall: ∂u/∂y ≠ 0. A fluid element embedded in this flow does not merely translate — it simultaneously rotates and deforms. The x-fiber remains nearly horizontal while the y-fiber tilts clockwise, revealing a net angular velocity Ωz ≠ 0. This motivates the question: how do we express Ωz in terms of the local velocity gradients?
§2 — The fluid element at t = 0
A rectangular fluid element at point O(x, y) in a 2D velocity field v = (u, v). The x-fiber OA (initially horizontal) and the y-fiber OB (initially vertical) are line elements embedded in the fluid that deform, translate, and rotate with the flow.
§3 — Two independent sources of angular rotation
∂v/∂x ≠ 0: The y-velocity is larger at end A than at O. End A rises faster, rotating OA counter-clockwise at rate  dα/dt = ∂v/∂x.
∂u/∂y ≠ 0: The x-velocity is larger at end B than at O. End B shifts right faster, rotating OB clockwise at rate  dβ/dt = −∂u/∂y.
§4 — Angular velocity in the xy-plane (step by step)
Consider the fluid element at t = 0. The x-fiber OA and y-fiber OB are aligned with the coordinate axes.
§5 — Summary
Angular Velocity
Ωz = ½(∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y)
Average rotation rate of two perpendicular fibers
Vorticity
ωz = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y = 2Ωz
z-component of ω = ∇ × v
Irrotational Condition
ωz = 0 ⇔ ∂v/∂x = ∂u/∂y
